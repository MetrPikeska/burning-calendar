## 1. Rules and judging 2026

### Judging criteria [J, CONFIRMED]
- Every judging level scores each criterion 1 to 5. No weights given.
- Impact: "addressing either a minor or major problem, which influences the quality and quantity of its overall effect." Score 5: "Its reach is extensive, inspiring, benefits a large audience, or could change the world."
- Creativity: "demonstrates originality and innovation. It introduces a novel concept that has not been attempted before or presents an improvement upon existing ideas in a meaningful way."
- Validity: "grounded in scientific principles and demonstrates technical, functional, or logical feasibility." Includes "usability and user-friendliness". Score 5: "fully developed and ready for real-world implementation."
- Relevance: "fully and directly addresses the challenge" and "meaningfully integrates NASA data, utilizing it as a core component ... rather than a superficial addition."
- Presentation: "clearly conveying the challenge, their proposed solution, and its significance." "Judges evaluate the demo and full project to compute this score." The guide tells teams to "pay equal attention to the final criterion: presentation."

### Submission [S, CONFIRMED]
- Window opens Sat 14 Nov 09:00, closes Sun 15 Nov 23:59, local event time zone. Late saves are not judged. Teams can resubmit; judges see the last saved version.
- Form fields: Project Name, High-Level Project Summary, Link to Project Demo, Link to Final Project, Detailed Project Description, Use of Super Intelligence (SI), NASA Data, Space Agency Partner Data, and Other References.
- Demo and final project must be public: "should not require a password, permission, or registration."
- Demo: video of at most 30 s, or at most 7 slides including title and intro slides. "Projects that exceed these limits will not be eligible for Global Judging."
- Video cannot be uploaded to the Space Apps site; host it elsewhere. Project page images: JPG, PNG, GIF, max 10 MB.
- Everything in English. Video may be spoken in another language but "MUST include English language subtitles". Slides English only. Slides must name the team, the members and the challenge.
- Global award eligibility needs at least one NASA dataset or resource.
- CONAE ("National Space Activities Commission of Argentina") is a 2026 Space Agency Partner.
- No likeness of anyone under 18.
- Video script by the second: [0:00] name and team, [0:02] challenge and why you chose it, [0:05] title and tagline, [0:10] how it works (images or prototype, user experience, how NASA and partner data enabled it), [0:20-0:30] future impact. Goal: understandable for someone without context or technical background.
- Recommends a FortiGuard Web Filter Classification for new sites, deciding the demo format early, and one person who owns the demo.

### Super Intelligence (SI) rules [S, CONFIRMED]
- SI is allowed, but "SI should not be used to (come up with your idea of how to solve the challenge)".
- SI output must not contain or alter NASA branding (logos, flags, mission marks).
- Images and videos: "Must include a visible watermark". Code and data: "Must acknowledge SI generation in descriptive text and metadata". Same for audio.
- Judges do not see the SI form field (data collection only). They do see and judge the acknowledgements inside the project: "originality, intent, and execution".

### Global awards, 10 categories [J, CONFIRMED]
Best Use of Science ("best and most valid use of the scientific method"), Best Use of Data ("best makes space data accessible or leverages it to a unique application"), Best Use of Technology, Galactic Impact, Best Mission Concept, Most Inspirational, Best Storytelling ("most creatively communicates the potential of open data through the art of storytelling"), Global Connection, Art & Technology, Local Impact ("greatest potential for making a difference in their local community").

### Advancement [J, CONFIRMED unless marked]
- Phase 1: local judges picked by the Local Lead choose Global Nominees; NASA announces them 3 Dec 2026. Local prizes are separate from global judging.
- Phase 2: Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions, 17 Dec 2026. Phase 3: NASA and partner agency executive panel, Global Winners 26 Jan 2027.
- Number of nominees per local event: not stated for 2026. 2022 guide: "1-3 solutions" depending on submissions; 2024 about one nominee per 10 projects [SNIPPET, https://spaceapps-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/Space_Apps_2022_Judging_and_Awards_Guide.pdf].
- Create Your Own Challenge not eligible for Global Judging [SNIPPET].

### Eligibility and prior work [J, F, L, CONFIRMED]
- Team of 1 to 6, all registered at the same local event, not waitlisted. No new members after submission closes. One team per person, one challenge per team.
- Submission checkbox: "all external resources including code, text and images (even if they are open source or freely available) used in the project are listed in the References field".
- No explicit ban on work prepared before the event found in L (full-text search).
- Original content made for Space Apps must be freely available or under an OSI licence.

## 3. Pitch and execution advice [CONFIRMED unless marked]
- Space Apps "240 Seconds of Glory": four quadrants of about 1 min, Who ("first 15 seconds are crucial"), Why ("if you can't do this in 60 seconds you're taking too long"), What (demo or prototype), How/Impact. https://2017.spaceappschallenge.org/blog/how-develop-winning-space-apps-solution
- Jono Bacon (judge): "Show something working within about 90 seconds." Avi Press: state the problem first; "be very direct about what works, what doesn't, how it works, and how it could be extended." Bonnie Xu: one "oh, this is possible now" moment, before vs after. Colin Lowenberg: a long demo is a scope problem; "Mock everything you can and make sure all your forms are filled." Jan-Niklas Wortmann: one finished thing over five half-done, read the rules and know the judges. https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/
- Estelle Chua (AngelHack): 3 min slot = 0:00-0:30 team, problem, one-line solution; 0:30-2:00 demo; 2:00-2:30 stack and hardest part; 2:30-3:00 next steps; 1-2 slides max. Record a clean run the night before, local fixtures instead of network calls, precompute slow steps, bookmark URLs, prefill data, disable sleep and notifications. Pick the presenter early, test the pitch on an outsider. https://angelhack.com/blog/10-tips-to-help-you-rock-your-next-hackathon-demo/
- Magomed Kurbaitaev (judge at 3 hackathons in 2026): open with "tool that does X for Y"; rehearse the first 30 s; screenshot plus 60 s screen recording for Wi-Fi failure; "Admitting a limitation raised scores in my sheets"; have an answer to "what breaks first"; no points for the stack. Common mistakes: too much context, stack talk eats demo time, vague user, unclear what was built at the event, heavy decks. https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l
- Terence Eden (hack day regular): premise in about 10 s, then happy path; stop coding about 2 h before presenting ("if you add anything new now, I guarantee you're going to break something"); rehearse aloud with a timer; test stage, mic, Wi-Fi; log in beforehand. https://shkspr.mobi/blog/2019/11/how-to-present-at-a-hack-day/
- QHack judges (Guillermo Alonso, Ivana Kurečić): "Scientific rigor and clearly and honestly show the results"; a flashier result without critical reasoning can lose to a simpler one; docs should let judges repeat the steps. https://pennylane.ai/blog/2024/02/how-to-do-a-great-hackathon-project
- Eugene Yan on Hacklytics: winners had a simple UI, often a deployed prototype; technical complexity and visualisation scored highest. https://eugeneyan.com/writing/how-to-win-data-hackathon
- Feature freeze 4 h before deadline, backup video as soon as the core is stable [SNIPPET, low authority; other blogs say 1 to 6 h]. https://blogs.reskilll.com/how-to-win-a-hackathon-15-tips-from-92000-team-registrations/
