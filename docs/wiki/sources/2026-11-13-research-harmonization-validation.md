The team's plan holds up against the literature and in places goes past it: a VIIRS-equivalent scale with a stratified scale factor fitted on the S-NPP overlap matches how GFED5NRT and the Punjab studies handle count continuity, and no published count study stratifies as finely (region x calendar month x day/night) or validates as strictly (leave-one-year-out). Two weaknesses need fixing before the harmonize step is final. First, the fitting window 2012-2025 is not stationary on the MODIS side: Terra lost orbit control in February 2020 and Aqua in March 2021, both satellites have since drifted about two hours from their nominal overpass times, and nobody has published how much that changed MODIS fire counts. A ratio fitted across 2020-2025 can therefore absorb a drift artefact and carry it into the 2000-2011 back-cast. Second, leave-one-year-out (LOYO) tests interannual stability inside the overlap but not the extrapolation itself, so the team needs a time-split test (fit 2012-2019, predict 2020-2025) and a direct trend test on the annual ratio. The calendar outputs also depend on the stratification in a way the team should state precisely: **a single constant factor leaves peak month, 10%/90% cumulative onset and end, and season length unchanged, but a factor that varies by calendar month (or a day/night split whose night share changes through the year) reweights the months and does move onset and end.** That is the point of month stratification and also the reason to validate the calendar metrics, not only the counts. The S-NPP to NOAA-20 continuation has no published count factor; the only numbers are a 12-day global daytime sample in March 2023 implying NOAA-20 at 0.988 of S-NPP, so the team should fit its own factor on the April 2018 to October 2026 overlap. Of the four claims checked, Kaur 2019 and Fu 2020 hold (with caveats on what they measure), Magi 2012 defines no onset rule and should not be cited for one, and no published S-NPP/NOAA-20 count factor exists.

## Published count continuity rests on stratified scalars and a single reference sensor

Peer-reviewed count harmonization between MODIS and VIIRS 375 m is thin and regional. The closest operational analogue is **GFED5NRT (Chen et al. 2026), which converts VIIRS fire detections (type 0 only) into the MODIS-era GFED5 record through an "effective fire area" per detection, fitted per 14 GFED regions x 16 land cover or burning types on 2012-2022** ([ESSD](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/7535/2026/)). The authors tested annual vs monthly and lumped vs type-specific aggregation and chose region x land cover; their scan-angle weights, built from S-NPP 2019-2021, are stratified by cross-track sample, day/night, month and land cover ([ESSD](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/7535/2026/)). The target is burned area, but the input is VIIRS counts, so it is in effect a count-to-MODIS-record calibration.

At regional scale, **Jethva 2022 regressed seasonal totals of S-NPP VIIRS 375 m on Aqua MODIS for Punjab and Haryana, 2012-2021, separately per crop season: VIIRS = 3.56 x MODIS + 18,836 for the rice season and VIIRS = 1.47 x MODIS + 11,404 for wheat**, with no day/night split and no monthly fit ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.1047278/full)). The large intercepts matter: they describe a population of small fires that VIIRS sees and MODIS never does, so a ratio through the origin is wrong there and the ratio changes with the size of the season. Vadrevu and Lasko 2018 (already in the team wiki) found VIIRS detected 4.8x more fires than Terra+Aqua and 6.5x more than Aqua alone in Punjab, with monthly FRP r² = 0.98 ([doi:10.3390/rs10070978](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs10070978)); a related poster gives 5.7x against Aqua (UNVERIFIED, snippet only) ([NTRS 20170011227](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170011227/downloads/20170011227.pdf), [LCLUC poster](https://lcluc.umd.edu/sites/default/files/KPV.pdf)). Fu et al. 2020 stratified Northeast Asia by forest vs low-biomass land and found MODIS omits most VIIRS fire pixels, worst on cropland and grassland ([doi:10.3390/rs12182870](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12182870)). For FRP rather than counts, Li, Zhang and Kondragunta 2020 fitted the median VIIRS/MODIS ratio per 1 degree of MODIS view zenith angle with RMA regression at seven grid sizes, and found MODIS underestimates daily FRP over Africa by at least 42.8% because it misses low-intensity fires ([NOAA repository PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/42501/noaa_42501_DS1.pdf)). The framing that still fits best is Csiszar et al.'s 2012 third continuity scenario: aggregated statistics from the two sensors are not compatible, but their patterns and trends are, and "explicit validation is crucial" ([NOAA STAR PDF](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/meetings/2012/AMS_NewOrleans_2012/Oral/2012_AMS_Csiszar_Fire_V1.pdf)).

Operational systems mostly avoid cross-calibrating counts. **INPE keeps one reference satellite for temporal comparisons, Aqua MODIS afternoon, and does not splice VIIRS into the reference series** ([INPE aviso 2011](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Publicacoes-Impacto/documentos/20110824_Aviso_Ref_Mudoup_Aqua.pdf), [TerraBrasilis change log](https://www.terrabrasilis.dpi.inpe.br/app/dashboard/fires/biomes/aggregated/data/historico-de-alteracoes.pdf)). When INPE moved from NOAA-15 AVHRR to Aqua in August 2011, it spliced without calibration and argued that Aqua counts were higher but kept "a mesma distribuição espacial e a mesma variação porcentual ao longo do tempo", except in north and northwest Brazil ([INPE aviso 2011](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Publicacoes-Impacto/documentos/20110824_Aviso_Ref_Mudoup_Aqua.pdf)). CAMS GFAS split its record into v1.2 (MODIS only, 2003-2025) and v1.4.2 (MODIS + VIIRS from 1 January 2026) with no reprocessing of the earlier period mentioned, so GFAS has a version break at 1 January 2026 ([ECMWF forum](https://forum.ecmwf.int/t/changes-to-cams-global-biomass-burning-emissions-based-on-fire-radiative-power-gfas-data-availability-on-ads-and-ecmwf-data-portal/14325), [CAMS](https://atmosphere.copernicus.eu/drifting-away-modis-cams-global-fire-assimilation-system)). A 2018 CAMS presentation reportedly describes a GFAS bias correction meant to keep the service stable if one or both MODIS instruments fail (UNVERIFIED, snippet) ([Kaiser 2018](https://atmosphere.copernicus.eu/sites/default/files/2018-11/5_181016_CAMSGA_CAMS44_Kaiser.pdf)), and a 2026 paper reportedly builds a daily FRP dataset at MODIS footprint scale from MYD14 + VNP14IMG with view-zenith and cloud corrections, FRP not counts (UNVERIFIED, snippet) ([doi:10.3390/rs18101650](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18101650)). The VIIRS products were designed after MOD14/MYD14 to support continuity (UNVERIFIED for exact wording) ([VNP14 User Guide V1](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/427/VNP14_User_Guide_V1.pdf)). EFFIS emission plots reportedly switched to v1.4.2 from 1 December 2025 (UNVERIFIED, snippet) ([EFFIS](https://forest-fire.emergency.copernicus.eu/about-effis/technical-background/fire-emissions)). The FIRMS FAQ offers no guidance on merging MODIS and VIIRS counts, only that "consistent counting of detections are essential" for trend work ([FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)). A planned calibrated 0.25 degree harmonized MODIS/VIIRS record (Boschetti et al. 2022) is a project plan, not results (UNVERIFIED, fetch failed) ([presentation](https://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/sci_team/meetings/202204/presentations/session1/boschetti.pdf)).

Other Space Apps 2026 teams are trying per-region OLS, quantile mapping per biome x season with a LightGBM back-cast, percentile matching, and cell-day presence instead of pixel counts (UNVERIFIED, self-reported, not citable evidence) ([firelens](https://github.com/shahmirkhan106/firelens), [nasa-flame-sense PR 2](https://github.com/Adedamola18/nasa-flame-sense/pull/2), [Mosharrof8097](https://github.com/Mosharrof8097/nasa_space_app_challange_2026), [firecal](https://github.com/samuelakosaonyejekwe/firecal)). Judges will see those methods, and no paper benchmarks ratio vs regression vs quantile mapping for fire counts, so a side-by-side comparison on the team's own data would be a genuine contribution.

## Nobody has cross-validated a count ratio or tested whether it drifts

Published validation of count harmonization is weak. **Jethva 2022 fitted 2002-2016 and held out one block, 2017-2021, reporting per-year percent error of 8.98, 1.52, 8.94, -0.74 and -14.05% for the scaled model, mean signed error 0.93% and mean absolute error about 7%**; the unscaled model did better, at about 4% MAE ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.1047278/full)). GFED5NRT reports Pearson r, RMA slope, normalized RMSE and Taylor skill score, with temporal TSS for burned area of 0.62-0.98 by region (global 0.88), but evaluates in-sample over its 132 fitting months and calls the numbers measures of "inter-product consistency and transfer errors rather than formal parametric confidence bounds" ([ESSD](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/7535/2026/)). Li, Zhang and Kondragunta chose RMA because it "minimizes errors of both dependent and independent variables" ([NOAA repository PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/42501/noaa_42501_DS1.pdf)).

The notes found no leave-one-year-out or k-fold cross-validation of a count harmonization, no formal test for a trend in the VIIRS/MODIS count ratio over 2012-2025, no published quantitative year-by-year ratio series, and no comparison of ratio, regression and quantile mapping for counts. The only statements on ratio stability are qualitative: Csiszar's "compatible general patterns and trends" and INPE's "same percent change over time" ([Csiszar 2012](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/meetings/2012/AMS_NewOrleans_2012/Oral/2012_AMS_Csiszar_Fire_V1.pdf), [INPE 2011](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Publicacoes-Impacto/documentos/20110824_Aviso_Ref_Mudoup_Aqua.pdf)). A snippet claims that over Africa MODIS and VIIRS counts differ strongly but peak burning month and season length agree in most areas (UNVERIFIED in the PDF text) ([NOAA repository PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/42501/noaa_42501_DS1.pdf)). The team's LOYO scheme with 2021 and 2023 as stress years is therefore stricter than anything published. Its blind spot is structural: every LOYO fold still trains on the drifted 2020-2025 years, so LOYO cannot detect a drift artefact that is common to the training set.

## Four mechanisms bend the ratio, and orbit drift sits inside the fitting window

### Orbit drift moved both MODIS overpasses by about two hours

**Terra's last inclination adjustment was on 27 February 2020 and Aqua's on 18 March 2021; the last drag make-up manoeuvres were 28 July 2022 (Terra) and 1 December 2021 (Aqua)**, after which the 16-day repeat ground track left its ±20 km box within months ([Twedt et al. 2023, NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001409/downloads/2023_SPIE_Twedt_MODIS_EV_orbit_drift_manuscript_v4.pdf)). NSIDC's 2021 projections put Terra at about 10:15 by October 2022 and about 9:00 around December 2025, and Aqua past 13:45 by February 2023 and about 15:50 around August 2026 ([NSIDC](https://nsidc.org/data/user-resources/data-announcements/ongoing-changes-terra-and-aqua-orbits-impacting-modis-snow-and-sea-ice-products)). terra.nasa.gov gives **8:32 AM MLT and 693 km for November 2026**, though the chart alt text on the same page says 9:59 and 692 km ([terra.nasa.gov](https://terra.nasa.gov/)); a 2025 NASA technical paper reportedly projects 8:30 AM by late 2026 (UNVERIFIED, snippet) ([NTRS 20250000522](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250000522)). A May 2025 Aqua status report reportedly predicted about 15:30 MLT in July 2026 and passivation on 4 November 2026 (UNVERIFIED, snippet) ([AquaStatus May 2025](https://aqua.gsfc.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/AquaStatus_May2025.pdf)); no 2026 primary source gives Aqua's current crossing time.

Giglio lists the drift effects on MODIS fire products: diurnal sampling, pixel size ("1 km → ∼970 m along-scan (spring 2025)"), scan gaps at nadir widening from about half to about 1¼ 250 m pixels by late 2026, bowtie overlap and narrower swath, with spatial sampling effects that "seem to have abruptly increased in magnitude in late 2024" ([Giglio, LANCE UWG, May 2025](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/filePost/LANCE_UWG_Orbit_Drift_Giglio_202505.pdf)). **His conclusion is that the impact on MODIS fire products "is under active investigation", and the January 2026 LDOPE issue still gives no fire-specific number** ([LDOPE issue 1254](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/displayissue?id=1254)). The direction is predictable from the diurnal cycle. In tropics and subtropics 90% of Terra+Aqua fire pixels are daytime, and in many regimes Aqua's afternoon pass carries most or nearly all of the signal ([Giglio et al. 2006, doi:10.1029/2005JG000142](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005JG000142)). Aqua MODIS records most fires at 13:00-14:00 local solar time, S-NPP at 12:00-13:30, and SEVIRI places the FRP peak at 14:00-15:00 ([Sofiev & Kouznetsov 2026, doi:10.5194/gmd-19-10123-2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-19-10123-2026)). Terra moving toward 8:30 should lose afternoon-regime fires with no change in burning, while Aqua first moved into the 14-15 h peak and then past it, so its counts relative to S-NPP can rise and then fall over 2021-2026. That is an inference, not a measured effect, and it is the main reason the ratio over 2012-2019 and 2020-2025 must be compared before the back-cast.

The team's Greek national series fits this concern without proving it: the yearly VIIRS/MODIS ratio ranges 3.10-4.01 and VIIRS/Aqua 5.57-7.17, **with both maxima in 2021** ([team analysis](../docs/wiki/findings-greece.md)). 2021 is both the megafire year and the first year of Aqua drift, so the stress year and the drift onset are confounded.

End-of-science dates conflict. Terra: December 2025 (LAADS and NSIDC, 2021 notices), January 2027 (CAMS), February 2027 (NASA Science). Aqua: August 2026 (LAADS, NSIDC, a 2025 Aqua status report, UNVERIFIED), September 2027 (CAMS, NASA Science) ([LAADS alert](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/alerts-and-issues/171807), [CAMS](https://atmosphere.copernicus.eu/drifting-away-modis-cams-global-fire-assimilation-system), [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/earth-science/terra-the-end-of-an-era/), [AquaStatus March 2025](https://aqua.gsfc.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/AquaStatus_March2025.pdf)). Wikipedia says that by June 2026 Aqua was expected to operate into September 2026 (UNVERIFIED, secondary) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqua_(satellite))). The newer sources give the later dates.

### Fire size sets the ratio, from about 1 for large fires to unbounded for small ones

MODIS routinely detects fires of about 1000 m², flaming fires of about 100 m² in very good conditions ([MODIS C6/C6.1 Fire UG](https://modis-fire.umd.edu/files/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf)). **The MODIS C6.1 FRP detection limit is 5.7 MW by day and 3.7 MW by night at the swath centre, about 42 and 39 MW at the edge; VIIRS C2 375 m reaches 3.7 MW by day and 0.7 MW by night at the centre** (0.5 MW in the abstract), and global omission in fire season is 20-40%, 10-20 points lower for VIIRS ([Sofiev & Kouznetsov 2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-19-10123-2026)). The 375 m product "provides much higher sensitivity to fires of relatively small size", while the VIIRS 750 m product is closer to MYD14 ([VIIRS C2 375 m UG v1.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)). Schroeder et al. detected a 4.9 m² bonfire at night at 32.7° scan angle ([Schroeder et al. 2014, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2013.12.008](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf)). Fu et al. found omission of VIIRS pixels by MODIS falls from about 65% at 2-4 MW per pixel to under 20% at higher FRP ([doi:10.3390/rs12182870](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12182870)). The practical consequence for Greece is that stubble-burning months and wildfire months carry different ratios, which is the physical case for calendar-month stratification. In fire clusters MODIS and VIIRS FRP are reportedly "very comparable" except in large boreal fires, where VIIRS FRP is about 47% lower (Li et al. 2018; UNVERIFIED, snippet) ([NOAA repository 49896](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/49896)). No paper publishes a count ratio as a function of fire size.

### Scan angle grows MODIS pixels tenfold and VIIRS pixels twofold

MODIS pixels grow from 1.00 km² at nadir to about 9.8 km² at the 55° edge (team calculation from the user guide formula; 9.3 km² at Terra's lowered 693 km) ([MODIS C6/C6.1 Fire UG §8.4.6](https://modis-fire.umd.edu/files/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf)). VIIRS I-band onboard aggregation and bowtie deletion keep the 375 m footprint between 383x360 m and 795x784 m ([Schroeder et al. 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf)). For the VIIRS 750 m product in early 2013, **VIIRS produced about 26% more detections than MODIS within ±31° and 70% more outside it** ([Csiszar et al. 2014, doi:10.1002/2013JD020453](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Csiszar_et_al_2014_JGR.pdf)). Monthly totals average over the 16-day repeat cycle, so for a fixed orbit the scan-angle mix over a region is roughly constant. Drift broke the repeat cycle in 2021-2022, so for a small region such as a Greek NUTS2 the scan-angle distribution can shift systematically after that, another argument for testing the late period separately.

### Night ratios run several times higher than day ratios

Against VIIRS 750 m, the 375 m product found **3x more daytime and 25x more nighttime fire pixels in August 2013** ([Schroeder et al. 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf)) and about 3x and 17x in the 2021 C2 global statistics ([VIIRS C2 375 m UG v1.2, §7](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)). In the guide's Lower Mekong example for 1 March 2016, S-NPP found 9,643 daytime and 4,608 nighttime pixels against Aqua's 361 and 22, ratios of 26.7 by day and 209 by night ([VIIRS C2 375 m UG v1.2, Fig. 4](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)). Per 1 km² pixel, VIIRS is about 3x more sensitive at night and about 25% less sensitive by day ([Sofiev & Kouznetsov 2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-19-10123-2026)). The Greek numbers follow the same pattern at lower magnitude: VIIRS/MODIS 3.14 by day and 4.50 by night, VIIRS/Aqua 5.08 and 11.30 ([team analysis](../docs/wiki/findings-greece.md)). A pooled ratio is biased high in months when large wildfires burn through the night. Argentina adds a complication: the South Atlantic Magnetic Anomaly covers the whole country, and the night filter that removes its spurious I4/I5 values also drops some small, low-intensity fires ([Schroeder et al. 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf)), so Argentine night ratios will differ from Greek ones for reasons unrelated to fire.

### Two data-version issues change counts before any fitting

**A bug in the Collection 2 monthly fire location files (Vxx14IMGML, versions 1 and 2) labelled many type 2 static sources as type 0 vegetation fires; NASA fixed it in May 2025 and reprocessed to version 3 (files named like VNP14IMGML.201801.C2.03.csv.gz)** ([VIIRS C2 375 m UG v1.2, §6.1](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)). A `type == 0` filter on pre-May 2025 files therefore keeps industrial hotspots and inflates the ratio near refineries and power plants. Collection 2 also removes the spurious "bad scan" detections of Collection 1 ([same guide, §2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)). The 2012 IDPS fixes (M13 aggregation bias fixed 3 April 2012, scan-line artefacts fixed 15 October 2012) concern the NOAA 750 m product, not the NASA C2 375 m record ([Csiszar et al. 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Csiszar_et_al_2014_JGR.pdf)); the first VIIRS active fires date from 19 January 2012 ([VIIRS C2 375 m UG v1.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)).

## NOAA-20 continues the record at 0.988 of S-NPP, on one 12-day sample

NOAA STAR treats the three VIIRS active fire products as one: **algorithms and formats are identical for S-NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 except the FRP coefficient (2.88 for S-NPP and NOAA-20, 2.95 for NOAA-21), and near-coincident data "showed high level of consistency between the three products"**, with a NOAA-21 FRP bias of up to about 10% ([NOAA-21 provisional readme](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/AMM/N21/NOAA-21_Active_Fires_Provisional_Readme.pdf)). The only count numbers found are daytime I-band fire pixels for 1-12 March 2023: S-NPP 430,815, NOAA-20 425,479 and NOAA-21 395,301, the last labelled "PRELIMINARY, NON-OPERATIONAL DATA" ([NOAA-21 beta review](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/AMM/N21/NOAA-21_Active_Fires_Beta.pdf)). **That implies NOAA-20/S-NPP = 0.988 and NOAA-21/S-NPP = 0.918**, a team calculation from a global, daytime-only, 12-day window, not a published factor. The 2018 NOAA-20 review lists "compatible detections counts and FRP retrievals" as a validation criterion and states "No precise match between Suomi NPP and NOAA-20" because the observations are about 50 minutes apart ([NOAA-20 provisional review](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/AMM/N20/Land_ActiveFires_Beta_Provisional.pdf)). The 750 m night algorithm is more conservative than the day one, consistently on both satellites ([same review](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/AMM/N20/Land_ActiveFires_Beta_Provisional.pdf)).

S-NPP FIRMS production "ceased at 15:40 Universal Time (UTC) on November 2, 2026" ([FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)); NOAA-21 joined FIRMS on 8 February 2024 ([FIRMS blog](https://wiki.earthdata.nasa.gov/spaces/FIRMS/blog/2024/02/08/343475617/NOAA-21+VIIRS+Active+Fire+Detection+Data+Now+Available)). NOAA-20 first light was 5 January 2018 ([NOAA-20 provisional review](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/AMM/N20/Land_ActiveFires_Beta_Provisional.pdf)). With algorithm differences ruled out, what remains is overpass time, data gaps and calibration. NOAA-20's earlier overpass sits further from the afternoon fire peak, so in regions with a strong afternoon cycle, such as Greek stubble burning or Argentine agricultural fires, the deficit can exceed the global 1.2%. The team has about 8.5 years of S-NPP/NOAA-20 overlap (April 2018 to October 2026) on its own data, which is better evidence than any published number.

## Month-varying factors move onset and end; a constant factor moves nothing

The definition the team plans, onset and end at the first period where the cumulative within-year count reaches 10% and 90% of the annual total, is **Freeborn, Cochrane and Wooster 2014's definition, with peak as the period of maximum count and duration as the number of periods between the two percentiles** ([doi:10.3390/rs6054061](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs6054061)). Freeborn used 16-day composites and chose a relative threshold because an absolute count threshold is "inappropriate" across products of different resolution. Giglio, Csiszar and Justice 2006 use a different family: peak is the calendar month with the maximum mean monthly count, and season length is the number of months whose mean count is at least 10% of the mean annual count, with cells under 5 fire pixels per year excluded ([doi:10.1029/2005JG000142](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005JG000142)). Giglio's 10% is a monthly share of the annual total, not a cumulative threshold, so the team should cite Freeborn for 10/90 onset/end and Giglio for peak month and active-month counts. Freeborn also notes a "core fire season" at 25th/75th percentiles, shorter than the 10/90 season.

The algebra is simple and the team should state it this way. If every month of a year is multiplied by the same factor r, the cumulative share of each month is unchanged, so **peak month, 10/90 onset and end, Freeborn duration, Giglio's 10%-of-annual season length and percent anomalies are all invariant under a single constant factor**. With a factor r(m) that varies by calendar month, the harmonized cumulative share becomes Σ r(m)M(m) / Σ r(m)M(m) over the year, which reweights months, so onset, end, length and even peak month can move. A day/night split has the same effect without any month stratification: the effective monthly factor is r_day x (day share) + r_night x (night share), and the night share changes through the season. Concretely for Greece, if spring and autumn agricultural months carry higher ratios than the summer wildfire months, the harmonized back-cast pushes onset earlier and end later than raw MODIS would show. This is what the team wants (the back-cast should mimic what VIIRS would have seen), but it means the calendar output depends on the stratification and must be validated directly.

Empirical evidence on how much these metrics shift between sensors is thin. **Freeborn found MODIS active fire vs ATSR active fire agreement on fire season duration "remaining less than 21% regardless of the grid cell resolution", and the more sensitive MODIS AF product "predominantly" gave an earlier start and later end** ([doi:10.3390/rs6054061](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs6054061)). Agreement improves on coarser grids and gets worse with longer time series. Giglio 2006 found Terra and Aqua monthly series cross-correlate at about 0.9 in most regions, but Aqua gives fire seasons 5 to 10 times longer than Terra in tropical rain forests, and a 20% cloud correction to counts produced "no significant differences in fire seasonality (peak month and season length)" ([doi:10.1029/2005JG000142](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005JG000142)). No study compares MODIS and VIIRS onset, peak, end or length directly, so the team's 2012-2025 overlap would be the first such comparison. The team already has one example: Thessaly peaks in October in MODIS and in July in VIIRS ([team analysis](../docs/wiki/findings-greece.md)).

Monthly resolution also limits what onset and end can resolve. With 12 bins, a 10% threshold lands in one month, and a shift of one month is the smallest detectable change, so validation should report onset/end differences in months together with the share of held-out years with an exact match.

## Greece and Argentina have almost no published sensor comparisons

No peer-reviewed MODIS vs VIIRS count comparison for Greece exists in the notes. The nearest is a preprint under review in NHESS: **Zervaki, Grillakis and Voulgarakis compared MODIS MCD64A1 burned area with Hellenic Fire Service records for 2002-2023 and found R² > 0.87 in 5 of 7 regions but a 41% national overestimate (12% on natural land cover), attributed to cropland burning in Thessaly-Central Greece and Macedonia-Thrace that the Fire Service does not record**, with the largest differences in September-October ([egusphere-2026-4576](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-4576/)). That supports showing agricultural burning as its own layer. Magi et al. 2012 describe the European agricultural fire season as "a minor peak in April followed by a dip and then a sharp increase moving into July" ([doi:10.5194/bg-9-3003-2012](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-9-3003-2012)), the shape where month stratification matters most. A Turkish study reports a June cropland peak in MODIS "not supported by any ground data" (UNVERIFIED, snippet) ([iForest](https://iforest.sisef.org/contents/?id=ifor3754-015)), and a global dating comparison reportedly found the highest date agreement in boreal and Mediterranean biomes (UNVERIFIED, snippet) ([ISPRS Archives 2024](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLVIII-3-2024/407/2024/isprs-archives-XLVIII-3-2024-407-2024.pdf)). The team's own Greek regional ratios, 3.25-3.78 across Central Macedonia, Thessaly, Evros and Attica ([team analysis](../docs/wiki/findings-greece.md)), are likely the only Greece-specific numbers.

For Argentina, **Morandeira 2021 counted 39,821 VIIRS S-NPP hotspots in the Paraná Delta in 2020, 39.8% of them in August, against 8,673 MODIS hotspots, a ratio of 4.59** (team calculation) ([ISPRS Archives](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLVI-4-W2-2021/109/2021/isprs-archives-XLVI-4-W2-2021-109-2021.pdf)). One month carrying 40% of a year's fires will dominate any anomaly against a 10-year mean, so log or median baselines are safer there. No primary CONAE MODIS/VIIRS comparison and no per-sensor Corrientes 2022 counts exist in the notes; Corrientes burned area figures vary by source, about 1,058,000 ha for January-March 2022 per SNMF via Chequeado and 934,238 ha by 21 February per La Nación (UNVERIFIED, snippet) ([Chequeado](https://chequeado.com/el-explicador/en-2022-se-duplico-la-superficie-de-hectareas-incendiadas-cuales-fueron-las-provincias-mas-afectadas/), [La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/economia/campo/sin-tregua-en-corrientes-ya-se-quemaron-mas-de-930000-hectareas-nid25022022/)).

**INPE has not switched its reference satellite. Aqua MODIS remains the reference in the 2026 notices, and the FAQ says S-NPP VIIRS will take over "quando o satélite AQUA deixar de operar", with compatibility "ajustada cautelosamente, pois o VIIRS detecta ~10 vezes mais focos que o MODIS"** ([INPE Queimadas FAQ](https://data.inpe.br/queimadas/portal/faq/)). INPE has published no adjustment method, and its FAQ named S-NPP, which itself stopped on 2 November 2026. INPE's notices record Aqua imaging gaps over part of South America on 10 July, 22 July and 12 August 2026, and state that no reference-satellite hotspot data exist for unimaged areas on those dates ([INPE Avisos](https://data.inpe.br/queimadas/avisos/)). Its reference history is inconsistent across documents: the 2011 notice says the reference moved from NOAA-15 to Aqua on 22 August 2011 with the Aqua series starting 10 August 2007 and NOAA-12 kept for 1998-2007 ([INPE aviso 2011](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Publicacoes-Impacto/documentos/20110824_Aviso_Ref_Mudoup_Aqua.pdf)), while the FAQ gives NOAA-12 until 3 July 2002 in one item and "até o 1o. semestre/2012" in another ([INPE FAQ](https://data.inpe.br/queimadas/portal/faq/)). In 2017 INPE reportedly processed S-NPP data as if they were Aqua, inflated reference counts, and replaced the records (UNVERIFIED, search summary) ([Infoqueima 2017-06](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Infoqueima/2017/2017_06_infoqueima.pdf)). INPE's "~10x" is an undocumented round number, far above the Paraná Delta 4.6 and Punjab 4.8-6.5, and should not be imported for Argentina.

## Recommendations for the harmonize step

**Method.** Keep the VIIRS-equivalent reference scale and the ratio as the primary method, computed as a ratio of sums (Σ VIIRS / Σ MODIS over the fitting years in a stratum), not a mean of yearly ratios, which explodes when MODIS counts are near zero. Fit two challengers on the same strata and report all three side by side: a Jethva-style linear regression with intercept ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.1047278/full)) or a log1p regression following Vadrevu and Lasko ([doi:10.3390/rs10070978](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs10070978)), and empirical quantile mapping of monthly counts, citing Kaur 2019 as the precedent for CDF mapping between viewing geometries ([doi:10.3390/rs11101205](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11101205)). Since nobody has published this comparison for counts, present it as the team's contribution.

**Fitting window and MODIS input.** Fit the primary ratio on 2012-2019, before Terra and Aqua lost orbit control ([Twedt et al. 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001409/downloads/2023_SPIE_Twedt_MODIS_EV_orbit_drift_manuscript_v4.pdf)), and fit a second on 2012-2025 to compare. If the two differ beyond the LOYO error, use the pre-drift fit for the back-cast, because 2000-2011 MODIS was observed at nominal orbits. Fit Terra and Aqua separately, or at least add a Terra-only ratio: Aqua data start in July 2002 ([FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)), so the November 2000 to June 2002 back-cast has only Terra and a Terra+Aqua ratio would underestimate it. Do not extend MODIS-based harmonization past mid-2026, since Aqua may have stopped and both orbits are strongly drifted.

**Stratification and fallbacks.** Keep region x calendar month x day/night as the finest level, and fall back hierarchically when a stratum is thin: region x month x day/night, then region x season (agricultural shoulder vs summer wildfire months) x day/night, then region x day/night, then national x day/night. A workable rule is at least 5 MODIS detections per year in the stratum, borrowed from Giglio's mask ([doi:10.1029/2005JG000142](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005JG000142)), with a minimum pooled total (for example 30 MODIS detections over the fitting years) before a month-level ratio is used. Night strata will fall back most often, because MODIS night counts in small regions are often zero. Never harmonize at 0.1° cell level: the team's cell-month log1p r² was 0.23 and 73% of VIIRS cell-months had no MODIS detection ([team analysis](../docs/wiki/findings-greece.md)). Treat Argentina's night ratios separately from Greece's because of SAMA filtering ([Schroeder et al. 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf)).

**Validation scheme.** Run three tests. LOYO over the fitting years with 2021 and 2023 (Greece) and 2020 Paraná Delta and 2022 Corrientes (Argentina) as stress years; note that 2021 is confounded with Aqua drift onset. A time-split test: fit 2012-2019, predict 2020-2025 VIIRS from MODIS, which is the one test that can expose a drift artefact. A trend test on the annual VIIRS/MODIS ratio per region and day/night (OLS slope or Mann-Kendall), which no published study has done. If the trend is significant, use the pre-drift fit or a time-varying ratio.

**Metrics to report.** On monthly counts: mean signed error in percent (bias), MAE, RMSE on log(1+count), Pearson r and the RMA slope of harmonized vs observed VIIRS, matching GFED5NRT ([ESSD](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/7535/2026/)). On annual totals: per-year percent error, directly comparable to Jethva's -14% to +9% range ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.1047278/full)). On the calendar, for each held-out year and region: peak-month hit rate, onset and end difference in months (Freeborn 10/90 definition, [doi:10.3390/rs6054061](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs6054061)), season length difference, and the same for Giglio's active-month count ([doi:10.1029/2005JG000142](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005JG000142)). Report raw MODIS vs observed VIIRS for those calendar metrics as a baseline, because that shows how much the month-varying factor buys.

**Splice and fallbacks.** Fit a NOAA-20/S-NPP factor per region x day/night on April 2018 to October 2026 and chain it after 2 November 2026; use 0.988 only as a sanity check, not as the factor ([NOAA-21 beta review](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/AMM/N21/NOAA-21_Active_Fires_Beta.pdf)). Measure NOAA-21 on its own overlap rather than using the preliminary 0.918. If the harmonized calendar fails validation for a region, fall back to INPE's approach: show percent anomalies and relative calendar metrics, which are invariant under a constant factor, instead of harmonized absolute counts ([INPE aviso 2011](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Publicacoes-Impacto/documentos/20110824_Aviso_Ref_Mudoup_Aqua.pdf)). Flag missing-data days such as INPE's Aqua gaps of July-August 2026 in the UI ([INPE Avisos](https://data.inpe.br/queimadas/avisos/)).

## Conclusion

The research shifts the team's main risk from sensor sensitivity, which month and day/night stratification already handle, to non-stationarity on the MODIS side. The 2012-2025 overlap looks like a long, clean calibration window, but its last six years come from drifting satellites whose fire-count effect NASA's own fire team has not quantified, and the team's Greek ratios peak in exactly the first drift year. A pre-drift fit plus a time-split test turns that uncertainty into a measured number, which is something no published count harmonization offers.

The calendar framing also changes what validation should prove. Under a constant factor, every relative calendar metric survives the sensor change untouched, so the only reason to stratify by month is to change the seasonal shape, and the only way to defend that change is to show that the harmonized MODIS shape matches observed VIIRS in held-out years better than raw MODIS does. If it does, the team has the first published-quality evidence of how MODIS and VIIRS fire seasons differ, for two regions where nobody has measured it; if it does not, the INPE-style relative calendar is a defensible product in its own right.
