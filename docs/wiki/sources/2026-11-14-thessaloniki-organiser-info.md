## Participant Onboarding FAQ (1 page), verbatim key parts

- On-site hours: "first day ... 9:00-21:00. During the second day ... 9:00-15:00. Work can continue off-site after the first (on-site) day has concluded."
- Submission: "submit your project on the NASA Space Apps Challenge Project Submission Webpage, by 11:59 pm on the second day of the hackathon. For the project's presentation to the judges, you should send the project to [email] by 15:00. Submissions past this deadline will not be accepted." The email address is a literal "[email]" placeholder in the PDF.
- Team Owners can edit and must resubmit to save changes until 11:59 pm on day two.
- Partial submissions are "eligible and encouraged".
- Non-NASA data allowed if the required NASA data from the challenge resources is also used; Space Agency Partner data optional.
- Language: "Your slides and/or video must be in English. The presentation can be in either Greek or English."
- Length: "approximately 7 minutes, with a short Q&A session afterwards, reserved for judge's questions."
- Food: lunch break and water dispensers.

## Presentation Guide (7 pages)

Logistics:
- "You have 7 minutes to pitch your project. This will be followed by a short Q&A session with the judges."
- Format: PDF recommended; PowerPoint or web slides (Canva, Google Slides) acceptable.
- Slides "should ideally be in English"; spoken Greek (most common) or English.
- Live demos: "If you have a working prototype, show it! However, have screenshots or videos ready as a backup. Your app must be deployed and publicly available."

Judging criteria (same five as the global guide, no weights or scale given): Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, Presentation. Presentation is judged "on their project page, in particular".

Recommended structure (explicitly optional): 1 The Challenge, 2 Your Solution, 3 Space Agency Data & Tech Stack, 4 Prototype / Demo, 5 Impact & Viability (audience, benefit, future steps), 6 Conclusion.

Tips: tell a story with a hook; less text, more visuals; do not spend 5 minutes on the problem and 2 on the solution; prepare answers for weak points before the Q&A.
